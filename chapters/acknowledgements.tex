This research was originally motivated by understanding the papers of Okayasu \cite{Okayasu2026} and Kian \cite{kian2026relative}, but then sprawled into a general study of the Arveson Radon-Nikodym theorem and how this can be used to study norm estimates of channel means. Throughout this process, the works of Bhatia \cite{bhatia1997matrix} and Hiai \cite{hiai2010matrix} have proven invaluable. Once the author noticed the problem with the geometric channel fidelity, stemming from the work of \cite{cree2020fidelity}, they decided to push just how far they could take this problem and the continuity estimates, discrimination and extending results to the hockey-stick divergences. Hence, during the preparation of this work the author used Claude (Anthropic; models Opus~5.5 and Fable~5.1). The purpose of this at first was to help to build a roadmap of the current literature which exists on the subject. Once trained on the material and what the author wanted to do using the ARN theorem, these models were used in studying the background literature, searching for references, suggesting proofs and statements, and suggesting edits to the exposition. This paper would not exist without the fundamental work of Arveson \cite{arveson1969subalgebras}, Raginsky \cite{Raginsky2003}, Kian \cite{kian2026relative}, Okayasu \cite{Okayasu2026}, Cree and Sikora \cite{cree2020fidelity}, Mosonyi and Hiai \cite{HiaiMosonyi2017}, and Watrous \cite{watrous2018theory}. Chapter~4 of this paper is the author's arxiv submission, the rest of this paper followed from the author's work with Claude over several months.
